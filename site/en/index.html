Ak-Tengir means “bright space”

The place for Ak-Tengir was chosen with care: mountains on one side and the pearl of Kyrgyzstan, Lake Issyk-Kul, on the other. That is why it feels so quiet, secluded and free here.

For its first ten years, Ak-Tengir was open by personal invitation only. Today its doors are open to everyone.

What matters most has not changed: silence, privacy and the feeling of a free, bright space far from the noise of the city.

Kitchen

From our land to your table

For more than ten years, our trusted team of cooks has been feeding Ak-Tengir’s guests. Every morning we bake fresh bread and pastries, we cook national dishes over an open fire, and in summer and autumn we preserve the vegetables and fruit grown right here at Ak-Tengir.

Our garden and permaculture

Our vegetable garden follows the principles of permaculture. It was laid out more than ten years ago by a specialist from Italy: the soil was built up in layers using natural felt and different types of earth. This keeps moisture in, preserves fertility and creates a natural environment for the plants.

Today we grow fresh herbs, aubergines, tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers and cabbage, and the orchard has apple, cherry and sweet cherry trees, and even a ginkgo biloba.

Most of what our land grows ends up on our guests’ table. We make compotes, drinks and smoothies from our own apples and local seasonal fruit and serve them at breakfast, lunch and dinner.

Who founded Ak-Tengir?

Jenishbek Nazaraliev is a psychiatrist and addiction specialist, Doctor of Medical Sciences, professor, founder of the Dr Nazaraliev Medical Centre and of the World League “Mind Free of Drugs”.

Read the story

Since the early 1990s, his work has focused on the human psyche, addiction and our capacity for inner transformation. But his search never stayed within the walls of a clinic.

Professor Nazaraliev travelled the world, learning from different countries and cultures. His path crossed five continents, from India and the Himalayas to Africa and South America. He studied medical approaches, spiritual traditions, and meditative and body practices of many peoples. Part of that journey became his book “Deliver and Forgive”.

All those years he was also looking for his own place: somewhere nature, silence and distance from the outside world could become part of a person’s inner work. That is how Ak-Tengir, the “Bright Space”, came to be on the south shore of Issyk-Kul.

The first ten years

Ak-Tengir remained a closed space. Guests and patients of Jenishbek Nazaraliev came here to recover, and it hosted psychological trainings and practices. Athletes, including Olympians, and business people from many countries came too. The Bright Space was open by personal invitation only.

Today

Today the founder’s family carries Ak-Tengir’s story forward. For the first time, its doors are open wider: to guests from Kyrgyzstan and travellers from all over the world. We carefully keep what this place was chosen for: privacy, silence, closeness to nature and a chance to step out of everyday life for a while.

We invite you to discover its story and philosophy, and this hidden corner between the mountains and Issyk-Kul, created with love and meaning.

What to see nearby

Ak-Tengir is a convenient base for exploring the south shore of Issyk-Kul. Canyons, mountain gorges, waterfalls and nomadic traditions are all close by.

  • Skazka (Fairy Tale) Canyon

    ≈ 8 km · 10–15 min

    Red and orange rocks, strange shapes and panoramic views of Issyk-Kul. Especially beautiful at sunset.

  • Barskoon Gorge and waterfalls

    ≈ 30–35 km · 45–60 min

    A mountain gorge with spruce forests, a river and several waterfalls. On the way you pass a monument to Yuri Gagarin. The waterfalls are about 15–20 km into the gorge from Barskoon.

  • Golden eagle show

    near Bokonbaevo · ≈ 40 km · 45–55 min

    A chance to see berkutchi, the Kyrgyz tradition of hunting with golden eagles. Shows are usually arranged in advance.

  • Jeti-Oguz

    ≈ 75–80 km · 1.5 hours

    The famous red rocks “Seven Bulls” and “Broken Heart”, and further on the Kok-Jaiyk mountain valley, walks and waterfalls.

  • Karakol

    ≈ 110 km · about 2 hours

    The main town of eastern Issyk-Kul: old wooden architecture, the Dungan Mosque, markets, cafés and Karakol’s famous cuisine.

  • Karakol ski resort

    ≈ 120 km · 2–2.5 hours

    One of Kyrgyzstan’s main ski resorts, among Tian Shan spruces. In winter: skiing and snowboarding, high-altitude runs and panoramic views of Issyk-Kul.
